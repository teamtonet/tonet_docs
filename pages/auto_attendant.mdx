# Auto Attendant

The Auto Attendant (also called IVR - Interactive Voice Response) is the phone menu your callers hear when they call in. "Welcome to Summit Plumbing, press 1 for Sales, press 2 for Service..." - that's an auto attendant.

You record a greeting, then map each button press to a destination - a [user](/users), a [group](/groups), another auto attendant, or an external phone number. Callers hear your greeting, press a button, and get routed where they need to go.

Go to **Manage > Auto Attendant** to see your auto attendants.

<Image src="/images/auto_attendant/ivr_list.png" alt="Auto Attendant list showing Main Menu with edit and delete buttons" width={1500} height={780} style={{borderRadius: '8px', border: '1px solid #e0e0e0', maxWidth: '100%', height: 'auto'}}/>

## Auto Attendant List

Each auto attendant is displayed as a card with:

- **Avatar** - The IVR's initials
- **Name** - The name you gave it (e.g. "Main Menu", "Sales", "After Hours")
- **Edit IVR** - Opens the editor to change the greeting, routes, or name
- **Delete IVR** - Removes the auto attendant after a confirmation prompt

Click **+ Add** in the top right to create a new auto attendant.

## Creating and Editing an Auto Attendant

The auto attendant editor has three sections: name, greeting audio, and routes.

<Image src="/images/auto_attendant/ivr_edit.png" alt="Edit IVR showing Main Menu with greeting audio player, Upload/Record button, and three routes configured" width={1100} height={700} style={{borderRadius: '8px', border: '1px solid #e0e0e0', maxWidth: '100%', height: 'auto'}}/>

### Name

A descriptive name for this auto attendant. Use names that reflect the menu's purpose - "Main Menu", "Sales Menu", "After Hours", etc.

### Greeting Audio

The audio message callers hear when they reach this menu. This is where you tell callers their options: "Thank you for calling Summit Plumbing. For Sales, press 1. For Service, press 2. To speak with the operator, press 0."

Click **Upload/Record Audio...** to open the audio dialog where you can:

- **Record** - Record a greeting directly from your browser using your microphone. Click the record button, speak your greeting, then stop and save.
- **Upload** - Upload an MP3 file you've already recorded. Drag and drop or click to select a file.

After recording or uploading, you can preview the audio with the built-in player before saving.

<Callout type="info">
  A greeting is required. You cannot save an auto attendant without recording or uploading audio first.
</Callout>

### Routes

Routes map button presses to destinations. Each route reads as: **Press [button] For [destination]**.

Available buttons: **1-9**, **0**, **Star (\*)**, **Pound (#)**, and **No Response** (what happens when the caller doesn't press anything).

Each button can only be assigned once - already-used buttons are greyed out in the dropdown.

Click **Add Route** to add a new route. For each route, select the button and then pick the destination. Click the trash icon to remove a route.

### Route Destinations

When you click the Destination field, the route picker opens with all available destinations:

- **[Users](/users)** - Route to a specific team member by extension
- **[Groups](/groups)** - Route to a ring group (the call rings all members, or queues if [call queue](/groups#call-queue) is enabled)
- **[IVRs](/auto_attendant)** - Route to another auto attendant for nested menus (see [Nested Menus](#nested-menus-routing-to-individuals) below)
- **Voicemail** - Send the caller directly to a user's or [group's](/groups#group-voicemail) voicemail
- **Phone Number** - Forward the call to an external phone number
- **Features** - Special routing options like Busy (play busy tone) or Repeat (replay the greeting)

Click **Save** to save your auto attendant.

## Connecting an Auto Attendant to a Phone Number

After creating your auto attendant, you need to assign it to one or more phone numbers so callers actually reach it.

1. Go to **[Phones](/phones)** and click the phone number you want to configure
2. In the **Configure** tab, set the **Go To** destination to your auto attendant
3. You can also set it as the destination in a [Schedule](/schedule) (e.g. play your main menu during business hours) or [Calendar](/calendar) (e.g. play a holiday greeting on specific dates)

See [Call Routing Priority](/phones#call-routing-priority) for how Tonet decides which destination to use when you have a calendar, schedule, and default route configured.

## Example: Routing to Groups

A plumbing company has two departments that receive calls: Sales and Service.

1. [Create two groups](/groups): "Sales" and "Service Team", each with the relevant team members
2. Create an auto attendant called "Main Menu"
3. Record a greeting: "Thank you for calling Summit Plumbing. For Sales, press 1. For our Service Team, press 2. To reach the operator, press 0."
4. Add three routes:
   - Press **One** for **Sales** (group)
   - Press **Two** for **Service Team** (group)
   - Press **Zero** for **Rick** (user - the operator)
5. Go to [Phones](/phones), select your main number, and set its destination to "Main Menu"

When a customer calls, they hear your greeting and press a button. If they press 1, the call rings everyone in the Sales group. If all Sales members are busy and [Call Queue](/groups#call-queue) is enabled, the caller waits in line. If everyone is offline, the caller gets the Sales group's voicemail.

## Example: Nested Menus (Routing to Individuals)

For larger teams, you can create multi-level menus by routing to other auto attendants.

Say your Sales team has three people and you want callers to choose who to speak with:

1. Create an auto attendant called **"Sales Menu"** with the greeting: "For Mike, press 1. For Sarah, press 2. For Jake, press 3."
   - Press **One** for **Mike** (user)
   - Press **Two** for **Sarah** (user)
   - Press **Three** for **Jake** (user)

2. In your **"Main Menu"** auto attendant, set the Sales route to point to the **Sales Menu** IVR instead of the Sales group:
   - Press **One** for **Sales Menu** (IVR)

Now when a caller presses 1 for Sales, they hear a second menu letting them choose the specific person they want. You can nest as many levels as you need.

<Callout type="info">
  **Groups vs. Nested Menus** - If you just want all Sales members to ring at once, route to a [group](/groups). If you want callers to choose a specific person, use a nested auto attendant. You can also combine both: "Press 1 to ring the Sales team, press 2 for Mike directly."
</Callout>

## Deleting an Auto Attendant

Click **Delete IVR** on any auto attendant card. A confirmation dialog asks you to confirm before it's permanently removed.

<Callout type="warning">
  Before deleting an auto attendant, check if any [phone numbers](/phones), [schedules](/schedule), [calendars](/calendar), or other auto attendants are routing calls to it. Deleting an IVR that's still referenced as a route destination will cause those calls to fail.
</Callout>
