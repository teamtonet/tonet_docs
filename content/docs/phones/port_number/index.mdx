Porting moves a number you already have from your current provider to Tonet. Your customers keep calling the same number.

Owners, managers and admins can start a port in **Manage › Phones › Port a Number**.

## Before you start

Get your latest bill from your current provider. You'll copy these from it:

- The account holder's name and business name, exactly as the provider has them
- The service address on the account (this can differ from your billing address)
- Your account number
- Your account PIN or passcode. You need it for wireless and VoIP numbers. Ask your provider for a port-out PIN if you don't have one.
- Every number you want to move

You'll also attach proof the numbers are yours: your latest bill showing them, a purchase receipt, or a screenshot of your account.

<Callout type="warning">
  Copy the details from a recent bill, not from memory. A shortened business name, an old address or a wrong account number is the most common reason a port is rejected.
</Callout>

## Start a port

1. Go to **Manage › Phones** and open the **Port a Number** tab.
2. Under **Account holder**, fill in the name, organization, service address, email, and **Residential or business**.
3. Under **Current provider**, enter the **Current service provider**, **Account number**, and **Account PIN / passcode**.
4. Under **Numbers to port**, add each number and pick its **Type**: **Landline**, **Toll free**, or **Wireless or VoIP**.
5. Under **Proof the numbers are yours**, click **Attach a file**.
6. Read the **Authorization** text. Add anything the porting team should know in **Additional information**.
7. Sign in the box, then type your name in **Print your full name**.
8. Click **Submit porting request**.

![The Port a Number form, starting with the account holder details from your bill](/images/phones/port-a-number-form.webp)

Not ready yet? Click **Save draft** and come back later.

<Callout type="info">
  Don't cancel your current service. Your numbers keep working with your current provider until the switch.
</Callout>

## After you submit

The **Port a Number** tab shows where your request is:

<PortTimeline />

| Status | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Submitted** | Tonet has your request and checks it before filing it with the carrier. |
| **In progress** | Being processed with the carrier. Most ports finish in 7 to 10 business days. The old carrier sets the pace, so no date is guaranteed. |
| **More information needed** | Read the message, then answer under **Messages** and click **Send reply**. You can attach a file. |
| **Approved** | The numbers show under **My Phone Numbers** once the transfer finishes. |
| **Not approved** | Read the reason, fix it, and click **Edit and resubmit**. |

When the numbers arrive, [choose where their calls go](/phones#choose-where-calls-go) and [who can use them](/phones#choose-who-can-use-a-number).

Want calls to reach Tonet before the port finishes? Ask your current provider to forward the number to your Tonet number.

## Why ports get rejected

| Reason | How to fix it |
|---|---|
| Name, address or account details don't match | Ask your provider for the exact details on file, then correct the request. |
| Unpaid balance with your provider | Pay what you owe them, then resubmit. |
| Wrong PIN for a wireless or VoIP number | Ask your provider for the correct port-out PIN. |
| The number isn't active | Ask your provider to turn it back on before you resubmit. |

Tip: ask your provider for your **Customer Service Record (CSR)**. It shows exactly how they have your account, so your details match the first time.
